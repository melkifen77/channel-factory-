## 3. Content Angles — What Works

### Proven Viral Hooks (Use These First)

1. **"Why you wake up at 3am"** — Highest engagement hook in the niche. Dr. Wei's version owns it via the TCM organ clock (liver time 1–3am).
2. **"What I stopped recommending after 40 years"** — Physician reveals they changed their mind. Applies to melatonin, sleep restriction.
3. **"Ancient Chinese medicine knew this 2,000 years ago (neuroscience just confirmed it)"** — High-share potential.
4. **"I'm 68 and I sleep better than I did at 38"** — Personal testimony hook.
5. **"The TCM explanation Western doctors miss"** — Positions Dr. Wei as bridge figure.

### Content Pillars

**Pillar 1: Sleep Science Explainers (15–20%)** — Neurochemistry, circadian biology, organ clocks.

**Pillar 2: Proven Techniques (30–35%)** — TCM acupressure tutorials (An Mian, Yong Quan), breathing methods. **Highest-engagement pillar. Tutorials drive saves → algorithm growth.**

**Pillar 3: Myth-Busting (15–20%)** — "I tested mouth taping for 30 days." Very high comment engagement.

**Pillar 4: Personal Narrative (20–25%)** — "What changed after I retired." "My grandmother's sleep remedy I ignored for 30 years." **Builds parasocial trust — the emotional layer that drives conversions.**

**Pillar 5: Soft Product Recommendations (10–15%)** — Framed as "what I recommend to patients." Day 60+ only.

## 4. Audience Insights

**Primary demographic:** 25–45, predominantly female (63%)
**Underserved demographic:** 40–70+, both genders, seeking non-pharmaceutical solutions

**Pain points:**
- Waking at 2–4am unable to return to sleep
- Unrefreshing sleep ("8 hours but still exhausted")
- Anxiety/racing mind at bedtime
- Medication dependency (want off Ambien/melatonin)
- Menopause-driven sleep disruption (massively underserved)

**Dr. Wei's core audience:** Health-conscious 40–65+ adults skeptical of pharmaceuticals, open to Eastern medicine. **This demographic has disposable income and high buyer intent. No current creator owns this.**

## 6. Risk Assessment

**Risk 1: AI avatar detection** — HeyGen avatars detectable by trained eyes. Lean into "educational" aesthetic. Early content should feel slightly imperfect.

**Risk 2: Platform policy on health claims** — Frame product mentions as personal experience ("what I take") rather than medical advice ("what treats insomnia"). Avoid sleep disorder claims without disclaimers.

**Risk 3: Dr. Breus has a head start** — Dr. Wei doesn't compete on his turf. Dr. Wei owns TCM + personal wisdom + older physician narrative. Different audience.
