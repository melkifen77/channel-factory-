## Full Script (TikTok, ~45s, ~140 words)

> I recommended melatonin to patients for 15 years. I don't anymore.
>
> Here's what changed. I had a patient — she'd been taking 5 milligrams every night for four years. Her sleep was still fragmented. I pulled her labs, and her own natural melatonin production was almost gone. Her body had stopped making it.
>
> I started looking for this pattern. It was everywhere. Long-term users had lower endogenous production, tolerance building within three months, and a dependency that looked a lot like the one I'd been trying to help them avoid.
>
> Here's what took me 40 years to understand: most insomnia after 40 isn't a melatonin problem. It's a cortisol problem. Your brain doesn't need more melatonin at night. It needs less cortisol during the day.
>
> I'm not telling anyone to stop anything. I'm telling you what I wish I'd understood 15 years sooner.
