His content sits at a precise intersection no competitor currently occupies: the rigorous credibility of a board-certified sleep physician, the intimate knowledge of a TCM-educated grandson, and the lived authenticity of a 68-year-old who has personally navigated sleep changes that come with age. Dr. Breus has the Western authority. Zoey Gong has the TCM. Dr. Wei has both.

The governing principle: **trust first, everything else second.**

- **Opening:** Must establish pain or curiosity in the first 2 seconds. No intro. No "Hi, I'm Dr. Wei." Start with the hook.

- Hook, then brief credibility signal is acceptable. "After 40 years treating sleep disorders..." works after the hook.
- Story arc (problem → insight → what to do). Personal anecdotes. Specific patient examples (anonymized).

### YouTube Shorts (up to 60 seconds)
- Slightly more patience here — audience has more intent. Teach one thing completely.

## Terminology Guide (Confidence: Medium)

### Must-Use Terms
| Term | Usage | Instead Of |
|------|-------|------------|
| "my patients" | When referencing clinical observations | "studies show" / "research suggests" |
| "in my practice" | When referencing clinical context | "clinically" / "in clinical settings" |
| "what I recommend" | For product/supplement mentions | "this product" / "you should buy" |
| "ancient wisdom" | When introducing TCM concepts | "traditional medicine" / "folk remedy" |
| "the research caught up" | When science validates TCM | "science confirms" / "proven" |

### Avoid These Terms
| Term | Reason | Alternative |
|------|--------|-------------|
| "What Western medicine won't tell you" | Oppositional framing undermines physician credibility | "What took me 40 years to understand" |
| "naturally" (standalone) | Vague, overused in wellness content | Be specific: "without medication" |
| "hack" | Implies a shortcut; undermines depth | "technique" or "method" |
| "optimize" (in sleep context) | Sleepmaxxing connotation | "improve" or "restore" |
| "cure" | Medical claim, legally problematic | "address", "improve", "support" |

### Never-Use Terms
| Term | Reason |
|------|--------|
| "miracle" | Supplement ad language; destroys credibility |
| "secret" | Conspiracy framing; undermines authority |
| "Big Pharma" | Adversarial; inconsistent with bridge-builder identity |
| "detox" | Pseudoscience; contradicts Western medicine training |
| Any patient diagnosis | "You have X" — legal and ethical boundary |

## CTA Progression Framework (Confidence: High)

### Phase 1: Days 1–30 — No CTAs
Content only. No product mentions. No link-in-bio calls.
Goal: Establish who Dr. Wei is before asking for anything.

### Phase 2: Days 31–60 — Identity CTAs Only
"Follow for more." Newsletter soft launch: "I put together a brief on the supplements I use — it's in the link if you want it."

### Phase 3: Days 61–90 — Soft Product Introduction
"Patients ask me what I take. I've been recommending magnesium glycinate for 20 years." Names the product, framing is personal recommendation. Link is in bio, passive.

### Phase 4: Month 3+ — Active Affiliate CTAs
"Link in bio for what I recommend to patients." Still framed as personal recommendation, never as advertising.

**CTA Language:**
- Phase 3: *"What I recommend after 40 years"* — not *"check this out"*
- Phase 4: *"The link in my bio goes to what I actually use"* — not *"use my code for 10% off"*
- Always: Mention one product at a time. Never a stack of links.

## Content Examples

### Excellent Example: TikTok Hook (30 seconds)

**Script:**
> "You wake up at exactly 3am. Can't fall back asleep. Your mind starts racing. Western medicine calls this sleep maintenance insomnia. Chinese medicine has been describing this exact pattern for 2,000 years — and calling it something different entirely. In TCM, 1am to 3am is liver time. If you wake during this window, your liver is working harder than it should be at night. Usually? It's stress you haven't processed. There's an acupressure point that can help. I'll show you tomorrow."

**Why it works:** Opens with the exact pain. Bridges both medical traditions. Builds curiosity for the next video. No product. No CTA. Pure credibility establishment.

### Example to Avoid: Early Affiliate Mention

> "Hey everyone, Dr. Wei here. So I've been getting a lot of questions about magnesium for sleep. I personally take magnesium glycinate and I think it's great, link in bio!"

**Why it fails:** Self-introduction before hook. No clinical context, no patient framing, no TCM integration. Phase 4 CTA delivered at Day 1.
